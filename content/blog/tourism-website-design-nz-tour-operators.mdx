*Last checked: 10 October 2026.*

The top result for "tourism website design NZ" is a 2016 blog post from a website builder. The next ones are American. None of them tells a New Zealand operator what a homepage needs in 2026, when travellers book on phones, compare you against your own platform listings, and increasingly ask an AI assistant before they ask Google. This is that list, written for owner run tour, activity, and cellar door businesses, followed by what a website costs in New Zealand this year.

**The short answer.** In the first screen on a phone, a tour operator's homepage must say what the experience is, where it starts, how long it takes, what it costs, and give a working booking button that keeps the traveller on your site. Below that: real photos, recent reviews, plain answers to common questions, current dates and notices, and the same facts that appear on your newzealand.com and Google listings. Most small operator sites miss three or four of these. A single page rebuild in New Zealand typically costs NZ$800 to NZ$2,000 plus GST from a generalist builder; tourism specialist agencies generally do not publish prices and work to larger budgets.

## The checklist

### In the first screen, on a phone

1. **What it is, in one line.** "Half day guided sea kayak trip in the Abel Tasman" beats "Welcome to our family business". The traveller decides in seconds whether they are in the right place.
2. **Where it starts.** Town or meeting point. Travellers plan around locations.
3. **How long it takes.** Duration is the second question every traveller asks.
4. **What it costs.** A "from" price is enough. A missing price sends travellers to your Viator or GetYourGuide listing, where the price is right there and the platform takes its commission. Our breakdown of [OTA commission in NZ dollars](/blog/viator-getyourguide-commission-nz-tour-operators) shows what that costs per booking.
5. **A booking button that works.** Wired to the system you already use (Rezdy, FareHarbor, Bookeo, Checkfront), opening on your site rather than sending the traveller elsewhere, and tested on a phone this week.

### Below the fold

6. **Your own photos.** Real boats, real guides, real weather. Stock photos are recognised instantly and cost trust.
7. **Recent reviews.** Two or three short quotes with a date and a link to your Tripadvisor or Google reviews. Undated testimonials read as old.
8. **Who runs it.** Owner run is a selling point. A photo and two sentences about the people who will meet the traveller.
9. **Plain answers to real questions.** Fitness level, minimum age, what to bring, what happens if the weather turns, where to park. These are also the questions travellers type into AI assistants, and an answer on your page is one they can quote.
10. **Trust signals in words.** "Qualmark Silver", "Department of Conservation concession holder", "operating since 1998". Written as text, not only as logos. Our note on [Qualmark fees and what they buy](/blog/qualmark-worth-it-small-tourism-operator) covers whether the first is worth it.

### Things that quietly lose bookings

11. **An old footer.** A copyright year from 2019 tells a traveller the site may be abandoned. Update it or remove it.
12. **Stale notices.** COVID banners, earthquake notices from years ago, last season's dates. If a notice no longer applies, take it down.
13. **Prices that differ from your platform listings.** Travellers check. Keep them matched.
14. **PDFs and images for key information.** A price list as a PDF, or a timetable as a picture, cannot be read by search engines or AI tools, and is awkward on a phone.
15. **Broken links.** Especially the phone number link (it needs the +64 format) and any links to Facebook or Messenger.

### For search engines and AI assistants

16. **The same facts everywhere.** Business name, location, experience, price, and booking link should match across your website, your newzealand.com listing, your regional tourism listing, Google Business Profile, and your booking platforms. Tourism New Zealand's own guidance is to treat your website as the source of truth.
17. **Structured data that matches the page.** LocalBusiness and Product or Event markup, with the same prices and details the page shows.
18. **Google Things to do.** Free listings in Google for tours and activities, if your booking system is a Google connectivity partner.

The full reasoning behind 16 to 18 is in our guide to [getting named in AI travel answers](/blog/ai-search-new-zealand-tourism-operators).

## Booking systems and Google Things to do

Your booking system decides how easy direct booking is and whether Google can list you. In October 2026 we read the websites of 76 owner run tour, activity and cellar door operators across both islands. Twenty two took no bookings online at all; travellers had to phone, email or fill in an enquiry form. Twenty more used their own booking page or one we could not identify. Twenty eight used FareHarbor or Rezdy, the two systems on Google's Things to do partner list. That was not a random sample (we were looking for owner run businesses, and set aside sites that had clearly just been rebuilt), but the practical point holds: a small operator on a partner system can be listed in Google for free, and many are not yet on one.

## What a tourism website costs in New Zealand in 2026

NZ web agencies that publish prices are mostly generalist small business builders. Their 2026 figures:

- **One page or landing site**: NZ$800 to NZ$2,000 plus GST (Kiwi Web Design, Auckland, updated July 2026).
- **Starter site of three to five pages**: NZ$1,000 to NZ$3,000 plus GST (same source).
- **Rebuild of an existing site**: from NZ$799 including GST with a budget builder (Impact Websites).
- **Tourism specialist agencies**: Auckland's tourism focused agencies do not publish prices; one lists its typical project budgets at NZ$5,000 and above.
- **Monthly SEO retainers**: NZ$1,500 to NZ$2,500 a month for a small business (Safari Digital, 2026).

The useful question is not which is cheapest but what the page will do for bookings. A NZ$800 template that keeps the booking button below the fold costs more in commission than it saves.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should a tour operator's homepage include?

In the first screen on a phone: what the experience is, where it starts, how long it takes, what it costs, and a booking button that keeps the traveller on your site. Below that: real photos, dated reviews, who runs it, plain answers to common questions, and trust signals written as text.

### How much does a tourism website cost in New Zealand?

Published 2026 prices from NZ builders run NZ$800 to NZ$2,000 plus GST for a one page site and NZ$1,000 to NZ$3,000 for a small multi page site. Tourism specialist agencies generally work to budgets of NZ$5,000 and above and do not publish prices.

### Which booking system works with Google Things to do?

Rezdy and FareHarbor are on Google's Things to do connectivity partner list. Bookeo and Checkfront were not on the list we checked in October 2026.

### Why do travellers book my tour on Viator instead of my website?

Usually because the platform shows a price, availability, and a booking button immediately, and your site does not, or your site looks out of date. Fixing the first screen of your homepage on a phone wins back many of those bookings.

### Does my website affect whether ChatGPT recommends me?

Yes. AI assistants summarise what they can read. Clear text stating your experience, location, duration and price, matching your other listings, makes it far easier for them to name you accurately.
