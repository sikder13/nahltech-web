Every build or buy article ends with "it depends." This one ends with four tests and a price, because the decision is simpler than the articles make it and the numbers on both sides are now public.

**The short answer.** Buy when the job is common to every business like yours (accounting, email, payroll, a website CMS). Build when the job is specific to how you make money (quoting, scheduling, intake, a customer portal shaped like your process), when you are already paying for three tools and a person to copy data between them, or when the off the shelf product makes you work its way instead of yours. The 2026 data says businesses are buying too much: median SaaS spend is $9,455 per employee and 36 percent of licenses go unused (Zylo, January 2026). A scoped custom build from us is $15,000 to $45,000, [published](/pricing), which for a 20 person company is between one and three months of its typical SaaS bill.

## What businesses actually spend on software they buy

Zylo's 2026 SaaS Management Index, built from more than 40 million licenses and $75 billion of spend, found the median company spends $9,455 per employee per year on SaaS, 36 percent of purchased licenses are not used, business units rather than IT control 81 percent of the spend, and 78 percent of IT leaders were hit by unexpected consumption or AI charges in the last year. Zylo's 2025 index put the average small company at about 152 distinct applications.

Those numbers describe larger companies, but the pattern scales down. A 15 person service business we talk to typically runs a CRM, a scheduling tool, a quoting tool, an e signature tool, a forms tool, and an automation tool to connect them, pays for seats nobody uses in at least two of those, and employs a person who spends part of every day retyping data between them. That person's time is the cost that never appears in the SaaS comparison.

## The four tests

**1. Is the job the same at every business like yours?** Accounting, payroll, email, calendars, document storage, a basic website. Buy. Nobody has a competitive advantage in payroll, and the vendors have spent decades on edge cases you have not thought of.

**2. Is the job how you make money?** How you quote, how you schedule crews, how you intake a patient, how you track a job through the shop, how a customer sees status. If the answer is yes and the off the shelf tool makes you change the process to fit it, you are paying twice: the subscription and the lost fit. This is where custom pays, and it is where our builds live.

**3. Are you already paying three tools and a person to connect them?** Add up the subscriptions and the hours. If the total passes about $1,500 a month, a $15,000 to $25,000 build that replaces the stitching usually pays back inside a year, and you own it afterwards.

**4. Will the vendor still be the right vendor in three years?** Price rises on renewal, features moved to a higher tier, an acquisition, an AI surcharge. Zylo found 78 percent of IT leaders hit by unexpected charges. Owning the tool removes the renewal conversation.

## The hybrid most small businesses should run

Buy the commodity layer. Build the one or two pieces that are your process, and have them talk to the commodity layer through its API. ## When not to build

When you have not run the process by hand long enough to know what it is. When the job is a commodity. When the budget is under $10,000, because a build that small is usually a spreadsheet with ambitions, and we will say so in the free scan. When nobody in the business will own the tool after launch. And when a well chosen off the shelf product covers 90 percent and the remaining 10 percent does not touch revenue.

## How to decide in one meeting

List every software subscription and what it costs. Mark each one commodity or process. For each process tool, write the one sentence of your workflow it makes awkward. Add up the hours someone spends moving data between tools. If the awkward sentences and the hours are both non trivial, get a fixed price for the build. ## Frequently asked questions

### Should a small business build custom software or buy SaaS?

Buy for commodity jobs (accounting, payroll, email). Build for the one or two jobs that are your actual process and that off the shelf tools force you to bend around, especially if you already pay three tools and a person to connect them.

### How much does a small business spend on SaaS?

Zylo's 2026 index puts median SaaS spend at $9,455 per employee per year, with 36 percent of licenses unused. Its 2025 index counted about 152 applications at the average small company.

### How much does custom software cost compared to SaaS?

Our scoped builds are $15,000 to $45,000, published. For a 15 person business paying $4,500 a month across five tools, a $30,000 build with $1,200 a month maintenance costs less from year two onward and removes renewal risk.

### What is the hybrid approach to build vs buy?

Buy the commodity tools, build the one workflow that is your process, and connect it to the commodity tools through their APIs. An AI automation build from $7,500 does exactly this.

### When is building the wrong choice?

When the job is a commodity, the budget is under $10,000, nobody will own the tool after launch, or an off the shelf product already covers everything that touches revenue.
