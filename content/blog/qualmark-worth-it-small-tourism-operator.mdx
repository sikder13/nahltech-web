*Last checked: 10 October 2026. Fees exclude GST.*

Qualmark is Tourism New Zealand's official quality assurance programme, and for a small operator the question is a practical one: is the annual fee repaid in bookings? Most pages that rank for "is Qualmark worth it" are either years old, published by Qualmark's partners, or about a different company altogether. This is a plain account of what it costs in 2026, what it changes, and how to decide.

**The short answer.** For a business turning over less than NZ$3 million, Qualmark costs NZ$950 a year plus a one off NZ$300 assessment fee for new applicants from April 2026. The most concrete benefit is on newzealand.com, where listings are ordered by Qualmark rating by default. Qualmark says its businesses get almost four times more referrals from newzealand.com; a regional tourism organisation quotes three times. If newzealand.com and inbound international travellers matter to your business, and you already run to a good standard, it is likely worth it. If your bookings come mostly from locals, repeat customers, and word of mouth, it is harder to justify on referrals alone.

## What it costs in 2026

Qualmark's published annual membership fees, excluding GST, last raised on 1 July 2025:

| Annual turnover | Annual fee |
|---|---|
| Under NZ$3 million | NZ$950 |
| NZ$3 million to NZ$10 million | NZ$1,545 |
| NZ$10 million to NZ$20 million | NZ$2,060 |
| Over NZ$20 million | NZ$5,150 |

New applicants also pay a one off NZ$300 assessment fee from 1 April 2026. Almost every owner run tour and activity business falls in the first tier.

The less visible cost is time. The first assessment is in person, with a Qualmark specialist, against the Sustainable Tourism Business Standards; reviews after that are online from April 2026, and they are annual. Expect to document health and safety, sustainability practices, and customer processes. If those documents do not exist yet, writing them is real work, though most of it is work a well run business should have done anyway.

## What it changes

**Ranking on newzealand.com.** Tourism New Zealand's own listing tips state that listings are ordered by Qualmark rating by default. For an operator in a busy region, appearing above competitors on the national tourism site is the most tangible benefit.

**Referrals from newzealand.com.** Qualmark's website says Qualmark businesses get "almost 4 times more referrals from newzealand.com than non-Qualmark businesses." The page is undated and does not publish the underlying data. Hamilton and Waikato Tourism's industry page says "3x as many referrals". Both figures point the same way; neither can be checked independently, so treat them as the programme's own claims.

**A trust signal travellers and trade can read.** The Qualmark Bronze, Silver and Gold marks are recognised by inbound tour wholesalers and some international travellers. Write your Qualmark rating on your website as text as well as showing the logo; Tourism New Zealand's AI guidance notes that trust signals stated in words are easier for AI tools to read than logos alone.

**A sustainability standard recognised internationally.** The standards are recognised by the Global Sustainable Tourism Council, which matters if you sell through inbound operators or to markets that ask.

## How to decide

Ask three questions.

1. **Where do your bookings come from?** If a meaningful share of your international guests find you through newzealand.com, regional tourism sites, or inbound agents, ranking and trade recognition pay. If most of your guests are domestic, local, or repeat, the referral benefit is smaller.
2. **How many extra bookings would repay NZ$950?** On a NZ$150 experience, about seven direct bookings a year repay the fee. On a NZ$400 charter, three. If you think Qualmark's ranking on newzealand.com could plausibly bring in that many, the maths works.
3. **Would the assessment make your business better anyway?** Many operators find the health and safety and sustainability work useful in its own right. If you would do that work regardless, the fee buys the mark and the ranking.

One more point. Qualmark improves where you appear; it does not improve what travellers find when they click through. A Gold rated listing that leads to a website with no prices and a broken booking button loses the booking anyway, often to a platform listing that takes 20 to 30 percent. ## Frequently asked questions

### How much does Qualmark cost in 2026?

NZ$950 a year excluding GST for businesses under NZ$3 million turnover, rising to NZ$5,150 above NZ$20 million, plus a one off NZ$300 assessment fee for new applicants from 1 April 2026.

### Does Qualmark help my newzealand.com ranking?

Yes. Tourism New Zealand's listing guidance says listings are ordered by Qualmark rating by default.

### How many more referrals do Qualmark businesses get?

Qualmark says almost four times more from newzealand.com; Hamilton and Waikato Tourism says three times. Neither publishes the underlying data, so read them as the programme's own claims.

### Do I need Qualmark to list on newzealand.com?

No. A listing in Tourism New Zealand's Tourism Business Database is free and open to eligible accommodation, activity, transport and tour businesses without Qualmark.

### Is Qualmark worth it for a very small operator?

It depends on how many of your guests come through newzealand.com and the travel trade. At NZ$950 a year, a NZ$150 experience needs about seven extra bookings a year to repay it.
