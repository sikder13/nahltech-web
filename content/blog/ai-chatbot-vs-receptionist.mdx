The verdict first: **if the job is answering the same twenty questions and capturing leads after hours, a chatbot wins on cost by a factor of fifty or more. If the job is judgment — calming an unhappy customer, negotiating, handling the unexpected — a human wins, and the chatbot's job is to know when to hand off.**

Most businesses need some of both. The mistake is paying human prices for robot work, or expecting robot prices to buy human judgment. Here's the actual math.

## The options, priced side by side

| Option | Typical 2026 cost | What it does well | Where it fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| DIY chatbot platform | $19–$99/mo (most small businesses land here, per the platforms' own 2026 pricing guides) | FAQs, basic lead capture, always on | You configure and maintain it; quality is what you build |
| Agency-configured chatbot | $3,000–$15,000 setup + platform fee | Properly wired to your booking, CRM, and site | Upfront cost; pick the right builder |

| Part-time receptionist | ~$18,000/yr (20 hrs/wk) | Judgment, warmth, flexibility | Covers 20 of your 168 weekly hours |
| Full-time receptionist | ~$36,000/yr salary before taxes and benefits (federal wage data puts the median right around there) | Everything a person does | Costs 30–60× a chatbot; still sleeps at night |

The number nobody puts in these comparisons: **a website gets visited 168 hours a week, and a human covers at most 40 of them.** Whatever else is true, the other 128 hours belong to software or to nobody.

## The math that actually decides it

Skip the vendor ROI calculators. Two honest questions settle this:

**1. What fraction of your inquiries are repetitive?** Pull your last fifty calls or emails. If thirty of them were hours, pricing, availability, "do you service my area" — that's robot work, and you're paying human rates for it (or worse, missing it after hours).

**2. What's a missed lead worth?** If your average job is $400, an after-hours chatbot that captures two extra leads a month pays for a $99 platform eight times over. If your average engagement is $40, it doesn't. The chatbot case is strongest exactly where job values are high and inquiries are repetitive — which is most service businesses.

The lesson from running one: **the AI is now the cheap part. The engineering around it is the product.**

## When the $49 platform is the right answer

Honesty section. If you need FAQ answers and a contact form with personality, a $19–$99 platform configured over a weekend is genuinely enough — start there, and spend the savings on whatever actually gets you customers. You don't need us for that, and anyone who says you do is selling.

## When hiring wins

If your inquiries are mostly nuanced — distressed customers, complex quoting, relationships — software handles the intake and the human does the work. And if you're at the size where a person answers the phone AND does the books AND schedules the crew, that hire is buying you three roles; no chatbot competes with that.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does an AI chatbot cost for a small business in 2026?

Platform subscriptions mostly run $19–$99 per month at small-business scale, per the platforms' own published pricing. Agency-configured setups run $3,000–$15,000 plus the subscription. Custom builds start around $5,000 and climb with integrations.

### Is an AI chatbot cheaper than hiring a receptionist?

On raw cost, dramatically — a $99/month platform is roughly 3% of a full-time receptionist's salary. But they're not substitutes: the chatbot covers repetitive questions and after-hours capture; the human covers judgment. Most businesses that "replace" a person with a bot actually redeploy the person to higher-value work.

### Can a chatbot really capture leads?

Yes, if it's built to — with an explicit save-my-details step, instant notification to a human, and a booking link. A chatbot that just chats captures nothing. Ask any vendor to show you the capture flow, not the conversation demo.

### What are the hidden costs of chatbot platforms?

Per-conversation and per-resolution billing. A plan advertised at $29/month can reach several hundred once AI add-ons, seats, and overages stack — a pattern the 2026 platform pricing guides themselves warn about. ### Should the chatbot sound like a human?

It should be useful like one and honest that it isn't. Ours says what it is, never invents facts, and hands anything involving money to a person. A chatbot pretending to be human costs you trust the moment it's discovered — and it's always discovered.
