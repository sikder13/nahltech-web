A growing share of your customers have changed how they search. Instead of typing "HVAC repair Indianapolis" into Google and scanning ten links, they ask ChatGPT or Gemini a question — *"my AC died, who should I call in Indianapolis?"* — and get back three names.

The question that should keep you up at night: **are any of those three names yours?**

You can find out in five minutes, free, using the test at the bottom of this post. First, here's what the published research says about your odds.

## What the data shows

**The shift is real and fast.** BrightLocal's consumer research tracked the share of consumers using AI tools for local business searches jumping from 6% to 45% in roughly a year. That's not a trend to watch — that's a channel that already exists.

**And most businesses aren't in it.** SOCi's 2026 Local Visibility Index looked at more than 350,000 locations across 2,751 brands and found ChatGPT recommending just 1.2% of them. In restaurants alone, 83% never appeared in an AI-generated local recommendation at all. Those were largely multi-location brands — the businesses with the most marketing resources behind them. Research on home-service brands specifically (by Geek Powered Studios) found that while 80% earn *some* AI citation, only 15% secure the top recommendation. However you slice the numbers, the pattern holds: **AI assistants name a short list, and most businesses aren't on it.**

**The mechanics explain why.** AI assistants don't search the whole internet fresh each time. They lean on a few things: what's in their training data, what their search partners index (ChatGPT leans on Bing's index — if Bing can't see you, ChatGPT usually can't name you), and what trusted sources say about you — reviews, directories, local press. A business can rank well on Google and still be absent from all three of those inputs.

**And there's a technical trap.** Most AI crawlers can't run JavaScript — Vercel's analysis of hundreds of millions of AI crawler requests found no evidence of JavaScript execution. If your website is a modern JavaScript shell, it can look perfect in a browser and be effectively blank to the systems deciding whether to recommend you.

## The 5-minute Indianapolis test

Don't take the statistics' word for it. Run your own business through this, right now:

**Step 1 (2 minutes).** Open ChatGPT (free version is fine — that's what your customers use). Ask it, in plain words, what a customer would ask: *"Who is the best [your service] in [your city or suburb]?"* and *"I need [your service] in Indianapolis — who should I call?"* Then ask the same two questions in Gemini.

**Step 2 (1 minute).** Count: did your business appear in any answer? Did your *competitors*?

**Step 3 (2 minutes).** Check the technical side: run your website through [Crawlmouse](https://www.crawlmouse.com), our free audit tool — it shows what AI crawlers actually see on your pages and finds the structural problems that keep businesses out of these answers. No signup.

Three outcomes, three meanings:

- **You appear** — genuinely good news. Now the job is keeping and widening that position, because these answers shift.
- **Competitors appear, you don't** — the worst square on the board. The channel works in your market, and it's sending your customers to someone else, today.
- **Nobody local appears** — the answers were generic or named chains. That's an open lane: in markets like this, the first independent business to build real AI visibility often becomes *the* answer.

No tricks, no "AI hacks" — the inputs are boringly concrete: a website AI crawlers can read (content in the HTML, not behind JavaScript), Bing indexation (five minutes in Bing Webmaster Tools to check), consistent name-address-phone everywhere you're listed, a healthy review presence on the platforms AI systems cite, and content that actually answers the questions buyers ask. ### How do I get my business recommended by ChatGPT?

Make yourself legible to its inputs: a crawlable website, presence in Bing's index, consistent business information, reviews on cited platforms, and content matching real buyer questions. There's no submission form and no shortcut — it's earned visibility, same as search always was.

### Does ranking on Google mean ChatGPT will recommend me?

No. The inputs overlap but aren't identical — published research keeps finding businesses that rank well on Google yet never appear in AI answers, often because of Bing indexation gaps or JavaScript-heavy sites AI crawlers can't read.

### How many people actually use AI to find local businesses?

Consumer research put AI-based local search adoption at 45% in the most recent tracking, up from 6% about a year earlier. It supplements Google rather than replacing it — which is why we treat them as two rails of one program.
