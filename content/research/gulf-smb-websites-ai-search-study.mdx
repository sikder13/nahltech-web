## The key numbers

- **24 of 44 sites (55%) have orphan pages** — pages an AI crawler can read perfectly but can never find, because nothing on the site links to them.
- **43 of 44 sites are fully readable without JavaScript.** The "AI can't see your site" problem barely exists here.
- **38 of 44 sites carry over-optimized anchor text** — the fingerprint of an older generation of SEO work.
- **Roughly one in eight sites couldn't be cleanly crawled at all** — refused, walled, or effectively empty to a crawler.
- **Median grade: B-.** Three sites earned an A-; none earned an A; two sat at D+.

## What we did

In early September 2026 we ran structural audits on 54 small and mid-size business websites across the Gulf — the UAE (the largest share), Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, Qatar, and Bahrain — concentrated in four web-dependent categories: company-formation consultants, dental and aesthetic clinics, tour and charter operators, and home services and fit-out firms. These are businesses whose customers arrive through search, and increasingly through AI assistants.

Each site was crawled by [Crawlmouse](https://crawlmouse.com), which maps the internal link graph the way a non-rendering crawler experiences it: what is reachable from the homepage, what is buried, what is orphaned, and what the site's configuration lets AI crawlers access. Sites came from our prospect research rather than random sampling — a deliberate choice we disclose plainly — and every metric below was locked in a written methodology before the first audit ran. 44 audits completed with full grades; the rest failed or returned near-empty crawls, which is itself a finding.

## The surprise: the Gulf's problem is not readability

The common warning says AI crawlers can't read modern websites because content hides behind JavaScript. In this sample, that is almost entirely false: 43 of 44 sites serve their content in plain HTML that any AI crawler can read. These are mostly WordPress builds, and WordPress renders readable pages.

The real disease is **reachability**. More than half the sites have orphan pages — real content, often expensive content, that nothing links to. A crawler that lands on the homepage and follows links, which is how AI systems explore the web, will never reach those pages. We found service pages, pricing guides, and long-form advice content sitting in this invisible state. One consultancy's detailed money guides — the exact pages a potential customer would ask an AI assistant about — were among the orphans. About one in four sites also buries reachable content deeper than three clicks from the homepage, where crawlers rarely go.

The pattern has a clear cause. These sites show heavy investment in an older SEO playbook: 38 of 44 carry keyword-stuffed anchor text at volume, and many publish content faster than they wire it into the site's structure. The result is a region of websites built to be read and never connected — readable but unreachable.

## Why this matters now

When someone asks an AI assistant to recommend a clinic, a formation consultant, or a desert safari, the assistant works from what its crawlers could reach. A site whose best content is orphaned competes with only the fraction of itself that is connected. And the businesses in this study live on exactly those queries — they are among the most search-dependent categories in the Gulf economy. The gap between the best and worst sites in the same category was wide: in one tourism cohort, the strongest site out-scored the weakest by more than twenty points on identical criteria, in a market where those companies sell nearly identical experiences.

## What a Gulf SMB should actually do

Three fixes cover most of what we found, in order of impact: connect the orphans (every page worth publishing deserves at least one real link from a page a crawler can reach); flatten the depth (anything a customer would ask about belongs within three clicks of the homepage); and retire the keyword-stuffed anchors in favor of links that say plainly what the destination is — machines and humans now prefer the same thing.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are Gulf business websites visible to AI tools like ChatGPT?

Mostly yes at the reading level — 43 of 44 sites in this study serve content AI crawlers can read without JavaScript. The failure is at the finding level: 55% have pages that no link points to, so AI systems exploring the site never encounter them.

### What is an orphan page?

A page that exists and works, but that no other page on the site links to. Crawlers discover the web by following links; a page without inbound links is invisible to that process, however good its content is.

### How were the websites chosen?

From our own research into web-dependent Gulf SMB categories — formation consultants, clinics, tour operators, and home services — across the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, Qatar, and Bahrain. It is a purposive sample, not a random one, and we say so. The metrics reported were locked in writing before any audit ran.
